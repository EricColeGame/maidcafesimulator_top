Everything above is advertised feature language from the developer's own store page, not a content list confirmed by playtesting. That distinction matters. ## The core loop, as described

Reading the advertised features together, the described loop runs roughly like this: you start with a neglected space, staff it with maids, take orders, and complete them by mixing drinks and serving customers. Money handling is a named mechanic — scanning receipts and counting change — which suggests transaction accuracy is part of the job rather than an automatic background process. Ingredient ordering happens by phone and arrives through a Maidroid courier, so supply management appears to be a deliberate step rather than something that restocks itself.

The three Maidchelin stars sit at the top of that loop as the long-term objective. What the page does not say is how stars are awarded, whether they are cumulative or evaluated in a single review, or what thresholds apply. If you want to know that, you have to play and observe.

## Practical management reasoning

Without published numbers, the honest advice is about method rather than values. The advertised loop gives you enough to reason about priorities.

| Advertised system | Reasonable first focus |
|---|---|
| Mixing drinks and serving customers | Speed and order accuracy before variety |
| Scanning receipts and counting change | Consistent transaction handling to avoid mistakes |
| Ordering ingredients by phone | Order before you run out, not after |
| Five maids with secrets | Staff coverage across peak hours first |

A few general practices apply to almost any cafe sim built this way. Track which part of your day produces the most orders and staff that window rather than spreading maids evenly. Keep your ingredient orders ahead of demand, since the Maidroid courier is described as a delivery step and delivery implies lead time. Treat the money-handling mechanics as a skill you practice early, because errors there tend to cost more than a slow drink. And approach the five maids as characters to spend time with, since the store page frames their secrets as a feature — that usually means progression is tied to interaction, not just to profit.

None of this guarantees three Maidchelin stars. The scoring rules are not public, so any claim that a specific routine will earn a given star is guesswork dressed up as a guide.
