## What the Steam page says you actually do

According to the official description, you inherit a rundown cafe. A kemonomimi girl arrives and changes things, and you end up running a maid cafe staffed by five maids, each of whom has a secret. Your goal is three Maidchelin stars.

The advertised activities break down into a clear shift loop:

- Mixing drinks
- Serving customers
- Scanning receipts and counting change
- Ordering ingredients by phone, with a Maidroid courier handling delivery
- Getting to know the five maids and their individual secrets

That is a management sim built around hands-on tasks rather than menus alone. ## A practical approach to your first shifts

Because the store page does not publish recipes or prices, the useful advice is about method rather than memorized numbers.

Start by learning one drink at a time. In first-person mixing games, muscle memory beats reading a list mid-rush. Repeat a single order until the steps are automatic, then add a second drink. This is general practice for task-based sims, not a documented recipe guide.

Treat receipt scanning and change counting as a skill to drill, not a formality. The official description lists both as core activities, which suggests mistakes have consequences. Slow down enough to be accurate before you try to be fast.

Watch your ingredient stock before a busy period rather than during it. The Maidroid courier system implies a lead time between ordering and delivery, so ordering late is a predictable way to lose a shift. How long that lead time is, and whether orders can fail, is not documented on the store page.

Talk to the maids between shifts. The five secrets are part of the advertised appeal, and relationship or story progress in management sims usually advances through repeated interaction rather than a single conversation. The page does not specify how secrets unlock, so treat any claimed trigger as unverified.
