Maid Cafe Simulator is a first-person cafe management game from developers Accelix and SCON, released on Steam on 7 October 2026. You inherit a rundown cafe, and the store description says a kemonomimi girl arrives and changes everything. From there, the pitch is straightforward: run the cafe, hire maids, mix drinks, serve customers, handle the register, and chase three Maidchelin stars while getting to know five maids who each have a secret.

That is the advertised shape of the game. This guide sticks to what the [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3232690/) actually states, flags what comes from community guides rather than the developers, and is honest about the many details the store page simply does not publish.

## What the official store page says you do

The verified feature list is compact, and it is worth reading closely because it tells you what kind of game this is. According to the Steam listing, you:

- Inherit a rundown cafe and bring it back into operation
- Staff it with maids
- Mix drinks and serve customers
- Scan receipts and count change
- Order ingredients by phone, with a Maidroid courier handling delivery
- Work toward three Maidchelin stars
- Get to know five maids, each carrying a secret

Notice how much of that list is mundane service work: scanning receipts, counting change, phoning in orders. This is not pitched as a pure visual novel with a cafe background, nor as a pure restaurant tycoon where staff run everything for you. The first-person framing and the register tasks suggest you are physically present behind the counter, doing the small jobs yourself. That is the core loop the developers chose to advertise.

## Mixing, serving, and the register

The drink-making step is the part most players will want specifics on, and this is where you need to be careful. The store description confirms that mixing drinks is a mechanic. It does not publish a recipe list, ingredient names, ratios, or a difficulty curve for any drink. Any page claiming to list exact recipes with exact values is working from play experience, not developer documentation, and should be treated as a third-party guide rather than a manual.

What is reasonable to expect, and how to prepare:

- **Read the in-game prompt before pouring.** Order screens in management games typically display the requested item; check what the customer wants before you start mixing rather than after.
- **Practise one drink at a time.** Until you know the in-game tolerance for mistakes, repeat a single order type instead of experimenting across the whole menu.
- **Watch the register step separately from the mixing step.** Scanning receipts and counting change are listed as distinct tasks, so treat them as their own mini-routine rather than assuming the game handles them automatically.

If you want the real recipe set, the only reliable source is the game itself: check the order screen, any in-game recipe book or notebook if one exists, and the first few tutorial shifts. A community guide can speed this up, but it can also be wrong or based on a different build.

## Ordering ingredients and the Maidroid courier

Ordering by phone with a Maidroid courier is one of the more distinctive advertised features. The store page confirms the phone and the courier exist; it does not confirm delivery times, ingredient prices, stock limits, or whether you can order the wrong quantity and waste money.

Practical approach, framed as general management practice rather than documented data:

1. Check your current stock before phoning, so you are not double-ordering.
2. Order early in a shift rather than mid-rush, on the assumption that deliveries take time. Verify the actual delay in your own save.
3. Keep a buffer of whatever ingredient your most common orders use, once you know which orders those are.

If the game has a visible stock screen or supplier list, that screen is your authority. Do not trust an article, including this one, over what the interface in front of you shows.

## Maidchelin stars: the stated goal, not a documented formula

Three Maidchelin stars is the game's stated ambition, a pun on the Michelin system. The store page gives you the target and nothing else: no scoring rubric, no breakdown of food quality versus speed versus decor, no threshold values.

That means the honest advice is to treat stars as a feedback signal rather than a checklist. Improve the things the game already asks you to do well, watch whether the star rating responds, and adjust. If a results screen or end-of-day summary shows which category lost you points, that screen is far more useful than any speculative guide. A player aiming for three stars should focus on consistency first: correct orders, correct change, and no empty shelves. Everything beyond that is speculation until the game tells you otherwise.
