Maid Cafe Simulator is a first-person cafe management game from developers Accelix and SCON, released on Steam on 7 October 2026. The premise is straightforward and a little strange: you inherit a rundown cafe, you hire maids, you mix drinks, you serve customers, and you try to earn three Maidchelin stars while getting to know five maids who each have a secret.

That combination of busywork and character drama is the whole pitch. The question most people ask before buying is simpler, though: what do you actually do minute to minute, and how much of the game is management versus story? Below is a breakdown of what the official store page confirms, what community guides add, and where the record goes quiet.

The official Steam listing is at [Maid Cafe Simulator on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3232690/).

## What the Official Store Page Confirms

The store description is the most reliable source for the game's identity, and it is specific about a few things:

- You inherit a rundown cafe.
- A kemonomimi girl appears and changes things.
- The cafe eventually employs five maids, and each has a secret.
- The long-term goal is three Maidchelin stars.
- Core tasks include mixing drinks, serving customers, scanning receipts, counting change, and ordering ingredients by phone from a Maidroid courier.

That is a management loop with a service minigame attached. Mixing drinks and serving customers are the moment-to-moment work; scanning receipts and counting change are the transaction layer; ordering ingredients is the supply layer that keeps the whole thing running. The maids and the Maidchelin rating sit on top as the progression and narrative spine.

What the page does not publish matters just as much. There is no full recipe list, no item price sheet, no maid stat table, and no code system. If you see a guide claiming to have "all recipes with exact values," treat it as unverified until you can reproduce it in your own save.

## The Daily Loop: Drinks, Customers, and Change

Based on the advertised features, a normal session likely follows a recognizable rhythm:

1. Check stock and order ingredients if something is running low.
2. Prepare drinks as orders come in.
3. Serve customers and handle payment.
4. Scan receipts and count change accurately.
5. Close out and reinvest in the cafe or the staff.

The receipt and change step deserves attention because it is the one task with an obvious failure mode. Counting change wrong presumably costs you money or reputation, though the store page does not state the penalty. General management practice applies here: keep a mental buffer of cash, do not over-order perishables, and do not spend every coin on upgrades the moment you can afford them. Those are habits, not documented mechanics.

Drink mixing is described but not detailed. There is no published recipe list, so any specific ratio you read online is either datamined, guessed, or invented. The safe approach is to learn recipes from in-game prompts and write down your own notes.

## The Five Maids and Their Secrets

The five-maid structure is the strongest hook in the marketing. Each maid has a secret, which suggests the game leans on visual-novel pacing between shifts rather than pure simulation. That is a reasonable read of the store page, but the page does not say how secrets unlock, whether they are tied to shift performance, or whether they affect the Maidchelin rating at all.

Third-party guides have begun appearing since launch, and some frame the maids as having distinct strengths. Per those guides, it is worth rotating staff rather than parking one maid on every task. That is a sensible habit in almost any management sim, but it is a community observation, not developer documentation. No official stat table exists, so you cannot currently verify which maid is "best" at anything except by watching your own service results.

## Earning Three Maidchelin Stars

Three Maidchelin stars is the stated end goal, and the name is a clear riff on restaurant star ratings. The store page does not explain the scoring criteria. There is no published breakdown of how drink quality, service speed, customer satisfaction, or maid relationships feed into the rating.

Because of that gap, the honest advice is process-based rather than numbers-based: serve accurately, keep the cafe stocked, avoid wasting ingredients on failed drinks, and pay attention to any in-game feedback after each day. Players who track their own results across several in-game days will learn the pattern faster than any guide can tell them, because the criteria are not public.
