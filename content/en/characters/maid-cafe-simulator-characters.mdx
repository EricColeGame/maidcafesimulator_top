## Running the Cafe: General Practice, Not Documented Data

Because no recipe list or pricing data is official, the honest advice is about method rather than exact values.

Count your change carefully. The game advertises receipt scanning and change counting as hands-on tasks, which means errors are probably possible and probably cost you something — patience, tips, or rating. Slow down on transactions until the input pattern is muscle memory.

Mix drinks in a sensible order. In any drink-mixing sim, batching similar steps reduces error, and the first-person framing suggests physical placement matters. If a customer waits, that's information about your workflow, not just bad luck.

Restock before you need to. Ingredient ordering runs through a phone call and a Maidroid courier, so there's likely a delay between ordering and receiving. Ordering at the moment you run out is a losing pattern in almost every management sim.

Watch your star rating as feedback, not a score. Three Maidchelin stars is the headline goal, so partial progress almost certainly reflects specific weaknesses — service speed, accuracy, or atmosphere. Test one change at a time so you can tell what moved.

None of the above guarantees three stars. It's reasoning from the advertised mechanics, not from a strategy guide.
