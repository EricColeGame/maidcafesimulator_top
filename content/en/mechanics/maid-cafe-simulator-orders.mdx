## What "not documented" actually means here

The practical consequence is that you should be skeptical of any list that presents exact recipe values, precise prices, or stat spreads as established fact. ## Managing a cafe without a published spreadsheet

Because the specifics are not documented, the sensible approach is to learn the game by playing it and by testing one thing at a time. General management practice for this kind of simulator is fairly consistent, and none of it depends on knowing hidden numbers.

Start by understanding the loop before optimizing it. Take an order, mix the drink, serve it, then handle the payment step. Scanning receipts and counting change are manual tasks here, which means errors cost you time and possibly accuracy. Slow down and get the routine right before you try to move quickly.

Treat ingredient ordering as a budget question, not a hoarding question. Ordering by phone through the Maidroid courier implies a delivery step you have to plan around, so ordering before you run out matters more than ordering in bulk. Watch how fast each ingredient actually gets used during a shift, and adjust from there rather than guessing on day one.

With the maids, pay attention to how each one behaves during service. Since the description says each has a secret, it is reasonable to expect that learning about them is part of progression, but how that interacts with cafe performance is not stated anywhere official. Experiment, and note what changes.

Spend time getting to know the five maids. In a game that advertises secrets for each of them, the story content is likely to be a significant part of the appeal, and rushing past it to chase stars may mean missing the point. Likewise, keep an eye on how the kemonomimi character fits into events, since the description credits her with changing everything.
