## How the core loop fits together

Because the verified feature list is specific, you can reason about the loop without inventing anything. The player is doing three kinds of work at once:

| Layer | Player action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Service | Mix drinks, serve customers | Direct income and reputation |
| Transaction | Scan receipts, count change | Accuracy and customer satisfaction |
| Supply | Phone orders with Maidroid courier | Keeps the menu available |

A first-person cafe game makes those layers tactile. Scanning a receipt and counting change is manual work in this framing, not a button labeled "complete sale." That changes the failure mode: mistakes are your mistakes, not the game's. General practice in this kind of sim is to slow down on the transaction step rather than rush it, because a wrong order or wrong change costs time and possibly reputation that you cannot get back in a single shift.

The supply layer is the one most likely to punish a new player. If you only order when you run out, you spend part of every shift waiting on a courier. A reasonable habit is to check stock after each shift rather than during one. That is general management advice, not a documented strategy from the developers.

The star system is the visible progress metric. The store page confirms three Maidchelin stars as the target but does not describe the requirements. Anyone claiming exact star thresholds is working from observation, not documentation.
