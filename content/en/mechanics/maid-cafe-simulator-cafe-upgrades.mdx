Maid Cafe Simulator is a first-person cafe management game from developers Accelix and SCON, released on Steam on 7 October 2026. If you have seen clips of drink pouring, receipt scanning, or maid dialogue and you are trying to work out what kind of game it really is, the useful move is to separate three things: what the developers advertise, what players report, and what nobody has actually documented. ## The advertised premise, in plain terms

According to the official store description, you inherit a rundown cafe. A kemonomimi girl shows up and changes everything, and the cafe becomes a maid cafe staffed by five maids, each of whom has a secret. Your long-term goal is three Maidchelin stars.

That framing matters more than it first appears. It tells you the game has two parallel tracks. One is operational: run the cafe well enough to earn recognition. The other is narrative: learn who these five people are. A management sim that only cared about efficiency would not bother giving every maid a secret, and a visual novel that only cared about story would not ask you to count change.

The store page does not state how long a playthrough takes, how many in-game days the star progression spans, or whether the secrets are optional content or required for the best outcome. Those are open questions.

## The hands-on loop players describe

The advertised activities are concrete and physical, which is unusual for the genre. The store description mentions mixing drinks, serving customers, scanning receipts, and counting change. It also mentions ordering ingredients by phone, with a Maidroid courier handling delivery.

Read that list again and notice what it implies. Scanning receipts and counting change are not abstract menu actions. They suggest a first-person interface where you physically handle objects at a counter. If you have played first-person job simulators before, you already know the texture of that: short tasks, repeated under time pressure, where speed comes from muscle memory rather than from upgrading a stat.

## Staffing and the maid roster

Five maids is a small roster by management sim standards. That is probably deliberate. A small cast means each maid can carry real characterization, and the "each has a secret" promise is easier to deliver with five than with twenty.

What the Steam page does not publish is any maid stat table. There are no documented skill values, no disclosed shift preferences, no confirmed affinity or relationship meters. Third-party guides may speculate about these, but speculation is not documentation. If you want to know whether a given maid is better at the counter or on the floor, the reliable method is to observe your own results in the game and adjust shifts accordingly. Treat any specific numbers you see quoted online with suspicion until you can reproduce them yourself.

## Working toward three Maidchelin stars

Three stars is the headline objective, and the store description frames it as an aspiration rather than a checklist. That is worth noting. The page does not disclose the criteria, the number of stars available at launch, or whether stars are awarded per evaluation period or once at the end.

Practically, management games of this shape usually reward consistency over bursts. Serving a few customers perfectly and then letting the till back up tends to score worse than steady, unremarkable competence. But that is general genre reasoning, not a documented rule for this game. You can verify your own progress by watching what the in-game evaluation actually reacts to, since the game presumably surfaces feedback when you earn or lose standing.
