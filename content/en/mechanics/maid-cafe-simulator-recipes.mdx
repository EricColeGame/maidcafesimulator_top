## What the Steam page confirms

## The three-part loop you are actually managing

Even without a published recipe book, the advertised systems suggest three overlapping jobs. Reading them as separate layers makes the game easier to reason about.

| Layer | Advertised activity | What it demands from you |
|---|---|---|
| Service floor | Mixing drinks, serving customers | Speed and order accuracy under time pressure |
| Front counter | Scanning receipts, counting change | Careful, repetitive attention to detail |
| Back of house | Ordering ingredients by phone | Planning ahead so you do not run out mid-shift |

The interesting tension is that these pull in different directions. Serving customers rewards moving fast. Scanning receipts and counting change punish rushing. Ordering ingredients sits outside the rush entirely and depends on whether you predicted demand correctly. A player who is fast on the floor but sloppy at the register will feel that in the numbers, and a player who never plans supply will eventually stall out on a busy day. This is general management reasoning, not documented data, but it follows directly from the activities the store page describes.

## How to approach your first few shifts

Since no official recipe or price data exists, practical advice has to stay general. A few habits tend to serve players well in this kind of simulation, and they map cleanly onto the advertised systems:

1. Learn the drink mixing interface before a rush, not during one. Muscle memory on the mixing step is what buys you time on the floor.
2. Slow down at the register. Scanning receipts and counting change are the advertised steps most likely to introduce errors, and errors cascade into unhappy customers.
3. Order ingredients before you think you need to. The phone ordering and Maidroid courier system implies lead time between placing an order and receiving it.
4. Talk to the maids. The secrets are a headline feature, and story content in games like this usually lives in repeated conversation rather than a single scripted scene.

None of this guarantees three Maidchelin stars. It is reasoning from the advertised design, and your actual results will depend on how the systems are tuned in the release build.
