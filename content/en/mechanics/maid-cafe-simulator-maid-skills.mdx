## What the game is, according to the developers

The store description establishes a clear premise. You inherit a rundown cafe, and a kemonomimi girl arrives and changes everything. From there the loop is a maid cafe: five maids, each with a secret, and a long-term goal of three Maidchelin stars.

The listed activities are concrete and mostly hands-on rather than menu-driven:

- Mixing drinks
- Serving customers
- Scanning receipts
- Counting change
- Ordering ingredients by phone, with a Maidroid courier handling delivery

The first-person perspective matters here. Scanning receipts and counting change are described as things you do, not as abstract buttons that resolve a transaction. That suggests the game leans on manual task execution and speed rather than pure spreadsheet optimization, though the store page does not quantify how demanding those tasks become.

The phrase "and more" appears in the description, which is a normal marketing hedge. It means the listed features are a floor, not a ceiling.

## How to approach the management loop without a guide

Because no official numbers exist, the practical approach is empirical. Learn the systems by running them and watching what the game actually rewards.

Start by treating the manual actions as skills. If scanning receipts and counting change are real-time tasks, then speed and accuracy compound across a shift. Practice the rhythm before you worry about expansion. A cafe that loses customers to slow checkout will not benefit from a bigger menu.

Second, respect the ingredient pipeline. Phone ordering with a Maidroid courier implies lead time. If delivery is not instant, running out mid-service is a self-inflicted problem. Watch how long orders take to arrive during your first few shifts and build a restock habit around that observed delay rather than a guessed number.

Third, read the maids as characters, not just units. The developers specifically advertise that each of the five has a secret. That is a narrative hook, and it suggests the relationship layer is intended to matter alongside the business layer. Paying attention to dialogue and shifts in behavior is more likely to surface those secrets than optimizing purely for throughput.

Fourth, treat the three Maidchelin stars as a long arc. The description frames it as an aim, not a quick unlock. Expect incremental improvement in service quality, consistency, and atmosphere rather than a single trick.

None of this guarantees stars. It is simply the sensible order of operations given what the game says it contains.
