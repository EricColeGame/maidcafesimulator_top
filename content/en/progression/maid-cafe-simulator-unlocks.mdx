## What the Steam Page Advertises

The official store description paints a fairly specific loop. You inherit a rundown cafe, and a kemonomimi girl arrives and changes everything. From there, the advertised pillars are:

- Running a maid cafe staffed by five maids, each of whom has a secret.
- Mixing drinks and serving customers.
- Scanning receipts and counting change.
- Ordering ingredients by phone, with a Maidroid courier handling delivery.
- Working toward three Maidchelin stars.

That is the advertised scope. Notice what is not there: no recipe list, no item prices, no maid stat tables, and no code system. The store page does not publish those, and any guide claiming exact values is either pulling from in-game testing or making things up. Treat the page as the authority on identity, and treat everything else as secondary.

## The Core Loop, Reasoned From the Description

Because the description lists mixing, serving, scanning, and counting change as separate verbs, the sensible reading is that these are distinct interaction steps rather than one merged action. In practice, that suggests a rhythm: take an order, build the drink, deliver it, then handle payment.

Two implications follow from that, and both are general management reasoning rather than documented data:

First, accuracy probably matters more than raw speed early on. If scanning receipts and counting change are modeled as their own steps, mistakes there likely cost you something, whether that is money, patience, or reputation. Until you confirm otherwise in-game, treat checkout as a task worth doing carefully.

Second, ingredient ordering by phone implies a stock system. If you can run out of something mid-service, then ordering ahead of a busy period is the safe habit. The Maidroid courier being called out specifically suggests delivery is not instant, so plan for a gap between ordering and receiving.

None of that is confirmed by numbers. It is the reasonable behavior a player should test in their first few in-game days.

## A Sensible Early-Game Approach

You cannot guarantee three Maidchelin stars by following a checklist, and anyone who says otherwise is overselling. What you can do is reduce avoidable losses while you learn the systems.

Start by learning one drink properly rather than rushing several. Since mixing is a core advertised action, getting a single recipe consistent gives you a baseline for how the game judges your output. Once one drink is reliable, add another.

Watch your stock before a rush rather than during it. The phone-ordering and courier delivery loop exists for a reason, and running dry mid-service is the kind of error that punishes you twice: lost sales and unhappy customers.

Pay attention to the maids as individuals. The store page explicitly says each of the five has a secret, which strongly implies their stories unfold through interaction over time, not through a single menu screen. If progression is tied to those secrets, then spreading your attention across the staff is likely more useful than fixating on one.

Finally, treat checkout as a skill. Scanning receipts and counting change are listed as activities in their own right, so it is reasonable to assume the game cares whether you get them right.
