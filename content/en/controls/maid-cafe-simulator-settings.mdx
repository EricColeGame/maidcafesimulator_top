## The Official Pitch, Straight From the Store Page

The [Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3232690/) describes a first-person cafe management simulator with a clear loop. You inherit a rundown cafe. A kemonomimi girl shows up and changes everything. You build the place into a maid cafe staffed by five maids, each of whom has a secret. The stated goal is three Maidchelin stars.

The advertised moment-to-moment work is concrete and hands-on:

- Mixing drinks
- Serving customers
- Scanning receipts
- Counting change
- Ordering ingredients by phone, with a Maidroid courier handling delivery

That list matters because it tells you what kind of game this is. Scanning receipts and counting change are not menu abstractions; they suggest the game asks you to physically handle transactions at the counter. The phone ordering plus courier suggests a supply step between serving shifts. The five maids with secrets suggests a narrative layer running alongside the management layer.

What the store page does **not** publish is just as important. There is no full recipe list, no item price table, no maid stat breakdown, and no code system. Anyone presenting those as documented facts is going beyond the official material.

## What "Scanning Receipts and Counting Change" Really Implies

This is the most distinctive advertised feature, and it is worth thinking through rather than skimming past.

If the game makes you scan a receipt and then count out change, the failure states are probably operational: a slow transaction, a wrong total, a queue forming. That is a different skill from optimizing a menu. It rewards accuracy and speed at the counter, not just knowing which drink sells best.

Practical reasoning follows from that. If your shifts feel chaotic, the bottleneck may not be your recipes at all — it may be your transaction handling. The way to check is to watch where your time actually goes during a shift: at the mixing station, at the register, or walking between them. The game does not publish a stat screen that tells you this, so observation during play is the only honest method.

## Building Toward Three Maidchelin Stars

The store page names three Maidchelin stars as the goal without explaining the scoring. That is a deliberate omission, and it means any article claiming to know the exact star thresholds is guessing.

What you can reason about safely:

**Quality and consistency likely both matter.** A star system named after a real-world dining guide implies evaluation of what you serve, not just how much you sell. General management practice — serve what you have practiced, keep the menu within what you can execute cleanly — applies.

**Staffing is probably part of it.** Five maids with individual secrets implies you will learn their dispositions over time. General practice: rotate who covers which role and pay attention to whether service quality changes, rather than assuming a fixed optimal assignment.

**Supplies gate everything.** Phone ordering through a Maidroid courier means running out of an ingredient can stop a shift. Checking stock before a shift is ordinary good practice, not a secret technique.

None of this guarantees stars. It is reasoning from the advertised design, and the game's actual scoring is something you verify by playing and watching what changes after each shift.

## The Five Maids and Their Secrets

This is the narrative hook, and the store page is explicit that each of the five maids has a secret. It says nothing about what those secrets are, how they unlock, or whether they affect the management layer mechanically.

If you are playing primarily for the story, the sensible approach is to spend time with each maid rather than optimizing them into a role and forgetting them. The game is described as letting you get to know them, which suggests interaction is available outside pure task assignment. How that interaction is triggered is not documented, so the way to find out is to try talking to each maid across multiple in-game days and see what changes.

Be skeptical of any list claiming to reveal the secrets in order. If such a list existed as official material, it would be on the store page or in developer posts, and it is not.
