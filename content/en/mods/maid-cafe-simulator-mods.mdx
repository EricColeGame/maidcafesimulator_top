## The core loop, as described

The game is first person and management-focused, which shapes how you should expect to play. You are not watching a spreadsheet; you are standing in the room doing the work. Based on the advertised features, a shift breaks into a few repeating phases:

First, prep. You take stock of what you have, then use the phone to order ingredients, which arrive via Maidroid courier. Running out mid-shift is the obvious failure state, so ordering before service starts matters more than ordering quickly.

Second, service. You mix drinks and serve customers. In first-person management games, the bottleneck is usually your own movement and timing rather than a hidden difficulty curve, so layout familiarity tends to matter more than raw speed.

Third, checkout. Scanning receipts and counting change is called out separately from serving, which suggests it is its own task rather than something that happens automatically. Treat it as a step you have to actively complete, not an animation that resolves itself.

Fourth, progression. Three Maidchelin stars is the stated goal, and five maids with individual secrets implies that staff relationships and story beats run alongside the business metrics.

## Management advice that does not require invented data

Because the store page publishes no recipe list, no item prices, no maid stat tables, and no code system, any guide that hands you exact numbers is either working from datamined files or making things up. Neither belongs in a planning article. What you can do instead is apply general practice and let the game tell you the rest.

Learn one drink at a time. In any mixing-based simulator, muscle memory beats a reference sheet, and a small reliable repertoire serves more customers than a wide shaky one.

Order before you need to. The Maidroid courier exists so you can restock without leaving the floor, but deliveries are not instant in any management game worth playing. Watch your shelves during quiet moments, not during a rush.

Count change deliberately. If counting change is a distinct advertised task, it is probably also a place where mistakes cost you. Slow down slightly at the register rather than trying to beat a clock you have not measured yet.

Watch the maids as people, not just staff. Five maids, five secrets is a story promise. If a maid's dialogue shifts, that is likely a signal worth following up on rather than background flavor.

Take the cafe's condition seriously early. Inheriting a rundown space means the environment itself is a variable. Improving the room is usually a prerequisite for raising your rating, not a cosmetic side project.
