## What the Official Steam Page Advertises

The verified store description for [Maid Cafe Simulator on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3232690/) is the only authority on what the game contains. It describes a first-person cafe management simulator in which the player inherits a rundown cafe, meets a kemonomimi girl who changes everything, and builds a maid cafe staffed by five maids who each have a secret. The stated goal is to earn three Maidchelin stars.

The advertised moment-to-moment activities are specific and worth listing plainly, because they define the genre the game sits in:

- Mixing drinks
- Serving customers
- Scanning receipts
- Counting change
- Ordering ingredients by phone, with a Maidroid courier handling delivery

That is the entire official feature set as published. There is no recipe list, no item pricing, no maid stat table, and no code system on the store page. If you have seen a guide claiming to list exact drink recipes or a redeemable code, that information did not come from the developers, and you should treat it as unverified.

## The Management Loop, Reasoned From First Principles

Because the store page names the actions but not their values, any practical advice has to stay at the level of general practice rather than documented data. Still, the advertised actions imply a recognizable rhythm, and thinking about it that way is more useful than memorizing numbers that may not exist.

Scanning receipts and counting change are the two steps that suggest the game cares about accuracy under time pressure. In most cafe simulators, that combination means the failure state is not running out of money but running out of patience — a customer who waits too long leaves, and a receipt that gets scanned late compounds the problem. Ordering ingredients by phone, meanwhile, implies an inventory you cannot improvise your way out of. If you run out mid-service, the Maidroid courier is your only restock, and delivery presumably takes time.

## The Five Maids and the Secret Storyline

The hook that separates Maid Cafe Simulator from a straightforward cafe sim is the cast: five maids, each with a secret, plus the kemonomimi girl described as changing everything. This is clearly the narrative spine of the game, and the three Maidchelin stars are the mechanical goal that presumably gates how much of each maid's story you get to see.

What is not documented is how those two systems interact. The store page does not say whether secrets unlock through star milestones, through repeated shifts with a specific maid, or through dialogue choices. It does not name the maids, describe their personalities, or hint at the nature of the secrets. Anyone presenting a "best maid" ranking or a list of endings is working from something other than official information.

The practical implication is that you should treat your first playthrough as reconnaissance. Rotate your shifts so you spend time with each maid, pay attention to when new dialogue appears, and note whether it correlates with your star progress or with something else. That is a strategy for learning the system, not a guaranteed route to any particular outcome.
