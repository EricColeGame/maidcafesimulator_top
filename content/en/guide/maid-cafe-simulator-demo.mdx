## What the Store Page Advertises

The [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3232690/) describes the core loop in concrete terms. You inherit a rundown cafe. You hire maids. You mix drinks and serve customers. You scan receipts and count change. You order ingredients by phone, with a Maidroid courier handling delivery. Your long-term goal is three Maidchelin stars.

The page also states that there are five maids, and that each one has a secret. That is a narrative hook as much as a gameplay one: the store description frames the maids as characters with backstory rather than interchangeable staff.

Notably, the store page does not publish a full recipe list, item prices, maid stat tables, or a code system. Anyone presenting exact recipe ratios, a price list, or a cheat code list as documented fact is going beyond what the developers have published. Treat such material as unverified unless you can confirm it in your own copy of the game.

## The Core Loop, Step by Step

Based on the advertised features, a typical session involves a repeating sequence. The order below reflects the features named on the store page, not a documented tutorial flow.

1. Open the cafe and take stock of ingredients.
2. Order what you are missing by phone, with the Maidroid courier delivering supplies.
3. Mix drinks to fill incoming orders.
4. Serve customers.
5. Scan receipts and count change at the till.
6. Repeat, reinvesting income into staff and stock.
7. Progress toward the three Maidchelin stars and the maids' personal stories.

The interesting design tension is that mixing, serving, and till work are all first-person tasks. That means your attention is split between production and administration. In practice, players in management sims of this shape tend to find that the till and ordering steps are where mistakes compound: a mis-scanned receipt or a miscounted change is a small error that repeats.

## Practical Priorities for a New Cafe

Because the specific numbers are not published, the sensible approach is to build habits rather than memorize values.

**Get the till right before optimizing anything else.** Scanning receipts and counting change are named features, and they sit between you and your revenue. If you are rushing service, slow down at the till. Inaccurate cash handling in a management sim tends to cost more than slow drinks.

**Keep ingredient ordering ahead of demand.** Phone ordering with a Maidroid courier implies a delivery step rather than instant restocking. If that is how it works in your build, running out mid-service costs you customers. Order before you are empty, not when you are.

**Treat the maids as characters, not just labor.** The store page states each of the five has a secret. That suggests interacting with them, and paying attention to what they say, is part of the intended experience rather than optional flavor. If you are purely optimizing throughput, you may be skipping content the developers built deliberately.

**Think of the three Maidchelin stars as a long arc.** Three stars is the advertised ceiling. It is reasonable to expect that reaching it requires the cafe to run well across service, staffing, and stock at once, not just to hit a single high number.
